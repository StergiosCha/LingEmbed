neuro-symbolic computational linguistics

Embedding linguistic theory
into neural representations

LingEmbed studies how the structures of formal linguistics (categories, types, λ-terms, dependency arcs, semantic graphs) can be written into vector spaces and neural networks, and how they can be read back out of them.

scope · 02

Linguistic theories as inductive bias

Each framework commits to a different kind of structure. Each commitment can become a constraint, a target, or an architecture for a neural model.

syntax

Categorial grammar (CCG)

Lexical categories such as (S\NP)/NP are functions. Derivations correspond to typed function application and composition.

Neural reading: categories as typed tensors, with combinators as learned or fixed contractions.

semantics

Montague & type-theoretic semantics

Meanings are typed λ-terms (e, t, e→t…), and composition is β-reduction. Modern type theories add dependent and subtyped structure.

Neural reading: type-respecting embeddings, and logic-aware losses for entailment and quantifier scope.

compositional distributional

DisCoCat

Pregroup types fix tensor orders: nouns are vectors in N, and transitive verbs live in N⊗S⊗N. Grammar specifies how the tensors are contracted.

Neural reading: grammar-shaped tensor networks with an exact diagrammatic semantics.

syntax

Dependency grammar

Head–dependent arcs labelled nsubj, obj, det. The analysis is a tree over the words themselves.

Neural reading: structural probes, graph encoders, and attention constrained by arcs.

meaning graphs

AMR & frame semantics

Rooted, labelled graphs of predicates and roles, e.g. read-01 :ARG0 student. Surface words that carry no content are abstracted away.

Neural reading: graph embeddings, role binding, and graph-to-text and text-to-graph models.

cognitive

Construction grammar

Form–meaning pairings at every grain size, from morphemes to idioms to argument-structure constructions.

Neural reading: constructions as reusable subspaces or memory slots, tested with minimal pairs.

interactive · 03

Tensor-product binding: structure in a single matrix

Smolensky's tensor product representations bind each filler (a word or concept) to a role (its structural position) with an outer product, then sum the bindings: T = Σ fᵢ ⊗ rᵢ. If the roles are orthonormal, unbinding T·rₖ returns fₖ exactly. Turn orthonormality off to see crosstalk. The fillers and roles come from the theory selected above.

    methods · 04

    Two directions of travel

    Neuro-symbolic work on language runs both ways. Symbolic structure can be injected into a network, or extracted from one that was trained without it.

    Symbolic → neural (embedding)

    • Tensor product representations: role/filler binding for trees and λ-terms.
    • Typed compositional models: grammar-determined tensor orders and contractions (DisCoCat; adjectives as matrices).
    • Logic as loss: Logic Tensor Networks and differentiable fuzzy semantics for quantifiers and entailment.
    • Structure-aware architectures: tree and graph encoders, and syntax-guided attention masks.

    Neural → symbolic (probing)

    • Structural probes: linear maps under which squared distance recovers parse-tree distance.
    • Diagnostic classifiers: decoding categories, types, roles and scope from hidden states.
    • Causal interventions: activation patching and interchange interventions on hypothesised variables.
    • Behavioural minimal pairs: agreement, NPI licensing, quantifier scope, and inference.

    project · 05

    Roadmap

    Planned components of the repository. Edit this list in index.html as the work progresses.

    Theory encoders

    Converters from CCG derivations, λ-terms, UD trees and AMR graphs to a shared role/filler format.

    Embedding library

    TPR, typed-tensor (DisCoCat-style) and tree-metric embeddings, with exact unbinding tests.

    Probing suite

    Structural, type and scope probes for pretrained language models, with control tasks.

    Benchmarks

    Compositional generalisation and inference tasks that compare theory-informed and unconstrained models.
